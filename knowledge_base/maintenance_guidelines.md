# Maintenance Guidelines for Rolling Element Bearings

## Preventive Maintenance Schedule

### Standard Monitoring Intervals
- **Continuous monitoring:** Critical machines (turbines, compressors)
- **Monthly inspection:** General industrial motors and pumps
- **Quarterly inspection:** Low-priority equipment
- **Annual overhaul:** Per manufacturer recommendation

## Lubrication Guidelines

### Grease Lubrication
- Re-grease interval depends on speed, temperature, and bearing size
- Over-greasing is a common cause of bearing failure (heat buildup)
- Under-greasing causes premature wear
- Use correct grease type specified by manufacturer
- Purge old grease during re-lubrication

### Oil Lubrication
- Check oil level monthly (or per maintenance schedule)
- Change oil annually or per analysis results
- Monitor oil temperature — elevated temp indicates bearing distress
- Oil analysis can detect metallic particles from bearing wear

## Inspection Procedures

### Visual Inspection
1. Check for grease leakage around bearing housing
2. Look for discoloration (blueing indicates overheating)
3. Check for corrosion on shaft and housing
4. Inspect seals and shields for damage

### Vibration Inspection
1. Measure overall vibration level (RMS) at bearing housing
2. Compare to historical baseline
3. Record trend data for degradation tracking
4. Perform spectrum analysis if overall level is elevated

### Temperature Monitoring
1. Measure bearing housing temperature with infrared thermometer
2. Normal operating temperature: 40–70°C above ambient
3. Alert threshold: 80–90°C above ambient
4. Critical: Bearing temperature rising rapidly

## Maintenance Actions by Risk Level

### CRITICAL Risk (Health Score 0–39)
**Immediate actions required:**
- Stop equipment if safe to do so
- Emergency inspection of bearing condition
- Check for visible damage, unusual noise, excessive heat
- Contact maintenance engineering team immediately
- Do not restart without inspection and clearance
- Prepare for bearing replacement

### HIGH Risk (Health Score 40–69)
**Schedule within 1 week:**
- Plan scheduled downtime for bearing inspection
- Prepare replacement bearing and tools
- Increase monitoring frequency to daily
- Document current vibration and temperature readings
- Review recent maintenance history
- Check lubrication status

### MEDIUM Risk (Health Score 70–89)
**Schedule within 1 month:**
- Schedule preventive maintenance at next planned stop
- Perform lubrication check
- Review operating conditions (load, speed)
- Continue standard monitoring
- Update maintenance records

### LOW Risk (Health Score 90–100)
**Standard schedule:**
- Continue standard monitoring interval
- Maintain lubrication schedule
- No immediate action required
- Document as baseline reading

## Bearing Replacement Guidelines

### When to Replace
- Bearing has reached end of calculated service life
- Vibration amplitude exceeds 2× baseline consistently
- Bearing temperature sustained above alert threshold
- Visible damage (spalling, pitting, corrosion)
- Excessive noise (rumbling, squealing, clicking)

### Replacement Procedure
1. Lockout/tagout the equipment
2. Document old bearing condition (photograph if possible)
3. Clean shaft and housing before new bearing installation
4. Check shaft diameter and housing bore dimensions
5. Use proper mounting method (heat fitting for larger bearings)
6. Apply correct lubrication before startup
7. Run equipment briefly and verify vibration/temperature
8. Record new baseline measurements

## Post-Maintenance Verification
After any bearing maintenance or replacement:
1. Measure baseline vibration in all axes
2. Record bearing temperature after 30 minutes of operation
3. Verify no abnormal noise or vibration
4. Update maintenance records and equipment history
5. Schedule first follow-up inspection within 2 weeks
